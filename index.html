Dictionary mark — design examples

Each row is the same paragraph. The whole sentence in the middle carries the 2px solid read-aloud underline (exactly where the app draws it). Inside that sentence, twelve saved words each carry a different candidate mark.

Judge at 100% first — that is what a reader actually sees. The 2× and 4× rows are only there to inspect the pixels.

100% — real reading size

Nobody had told me what the house would feel like once the last of the boxes was gone. The kitchen clock still ticked in the same place, and the stairs still complained under the same step, but the rooms had lost the smell of cooking and the sound of somebody else breathing in the next room. After school I spent whole evenings in room, sorting through the box of photographs my mother kept under bed, until light faded and everything went grey. For a long time afterwards I could not say whether that was grief or simply the ordinary quiet of a house that had been emptied out, and I decided in the end that it did not matter which.

Candidates

  1. Fine dots — 1px dots, 3px apart, 4px above the read-aloud line
  2. Coarse dots — 2px dots with wide 6px gaps
  3. Short thick dash — heavy 3px bar under the middle 40% of the word
  4. Long thin dash — 70% width, 1px — a whisper of a line
  5. Double rule — two 1px rules, 3px apart
  6. Gapped rule — 2px rule with a gap at each end
  7. Accent dots — same as fine dots but in accent blue
  8. Middle marker — 2px bar under the middle 60% only
  9. Overline dots — dots above the word instead of below
  10. Single dot below — one 4px dot centred under the word
  11. Footnote dot — no line at all — a small dot after the word
  12. End ticks — two short vertical ticks, one at each end of the word

Recommendation

At 100% size, anything that is a horizontal rule — dots, dashes, double rules, gapped rules — still reads as a second underline because it lives in the same 5px band as the read-aloud line. The two that survive at reading size are the ones that are not a rule at all: the footnote dot after the word and the accent dots. The footnote dot is the safest (no colour decision, no collision with the read-aloud line, works in vertical writing modes unchanged); accent dots are the most visible if a colour is acceptable.
