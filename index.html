Dictionary mark: candidates in context

Body text is 16px serif with line-height 1.4. The sentence being read aloud has the fixed 2px solid underline, drawn from the content-box bottom (baseline + 5px) downward. Each numbered word in that sentence is a saved dictionary word with a different candidate mark. Every mark is pure CSS on an empty 24px box whose baseline is 19px from the top, and stays in the 5px band between the baseline and the read-aloud line. Sizes are in em, so they scale with the reader’s font size. Literata isn’t bundled (no external fonts), so a platform serif stands in. The mark geometry is pinned to the baseline either way.

At reading size (100%)

The tide had gone out in the night, and by morning the harbour at Penhallow was a field of grey mud stitched with the bright ribs of stranded boats. Mara walked the length of the sea wall twice before she found the nerve to go down the slipway, her boots slithering on the weed. Her grandfather had always said that a quiet estuary keeps its secrets badly, giving them up to anyone patient enough to wait for the lowest spring tide and stubborn enough to dig through the cold, sucking silt. She had brought his old spade, a torch she would not need until dusk, and a paper bag of sandwiches she already knew she would not eat. Behind her, somewhere up in the village, a dog barked at nothing and was told to hush.

Recommendation

Reads as distinct from the solid read-aloud line at 100%: 12 (accent dots) clearly, 9 (three-dot cluster) clearly, 11 (wedge) and 6 (coarse round dots) mostly. 10 (single dot) is distinct but faint: easy to miss, and can pass for an ink speck or a stray full stop.

Does not: 1–4 fuse with the read-aloud rule into one heavy double line. 5 reads as a dashed second line and 7 as the rule thickening under one syllable. 8’s brackets are too small to read as brackets.

The pattern: anything that runs the full width of the word in this 5px band joins the read-aloud rule, whatever its weight or dash. What works is a short, centred mark (9, 10, 11) or a colour change (12). Spacing tweaks on a full-width rule do not.

  • Shortlist 9, the three-dot cluster. It is the best text-colour option: unmistakably a mark, the same size on every word length, and the dots go down the side unchanged in vertical writing.
  • Shortlist 12, accent dots, if colour is acceptable. It is the strongest separation. Colour can also go on 9 (an accent three-dot cluster), which would likely be the most legible of all.
  • Keep 11, the wedge, as the alternative to 9. It points at the word, but its shape only shows at 2x or more.

Caveats: this was judged in Chrome at 2x device pixel ratio with a fallback serif, not Literata on the device, so check the shortlist there. Any mark in this band crosses the descenders of g, j, p, q, y (see the g’s in 6 and 12), and a centred mark (9–11) will sometimes land on one. Ideas that leave the band (superscript badge, dots above the word) are not shown. A dot after the word in the band reads as a full stop, and a wavy rule reads as a spell-check error, so both were dropped.
